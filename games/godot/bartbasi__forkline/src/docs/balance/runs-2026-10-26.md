## Suggestions for G5

These are suggestions. I do not change rules or levels.

**Game designer and roguelite engineer**

1. **The every-card gap (-13.3 on Normal) is the one rule bar left.** Facts (F5): the six unlock perks cost 10.8 points together (Volley 3.5, Twin Pebbles 2.6, Long Fuse 1.4, the other three about 0). The economy unlocks cost 1.8, the risk unlocks 0.6. A bot that passes on every sidegrade still loses 8.0, from dilution. Options, untested:
   - Soften the three costly sidegrades: Volley's arrows lose 15% not 25%, Twin Pebbles loses 1 damage not 2, Long Fuse reloads 0.25 s slower not 0.5 s. This addresses the 7.5 points the singles carry. I would play it again with `pool=plus:...` (the what-if exists).
   - Keep the base cards' share of a draft when cards unlock, for example by offering an unlock card only in the slot that would have been a duplicate kind. This addresses the 8 points of dilution but changes RULES 14.4's draft rule.
   - Move the bar: a blind picker takes a sidegrade as often as a good card, so the bots' floor is lower than a player's. 10 to 13 points would hold today. The bar was written to keep unlocks from helping or hurting (brief 3.7), so this is the designer's call.
2. **Deep Lullaby +5.8 with every card** rests on 162 offers (standard error 1.9). I would not change it. If it repeats in the next experiment, the card could lose 5 points of slow (45% to 40%).
3. **Late gold is still idle:** 3,659 median unspent at wave 18, nothing to buy from wave 16 in every play. The boss budget idea of G3 1.3 (sell and rebuild toward the boss) is not something the bots do. One cheap lever is the late clear bonus. This is not a bar.
4. **Boss aiming (F10)** is worth 12.5 points to the bots and aimed bots reach 82% with the best strategy. If Bart wants Normal to stay hard for players who aim and read the preview, the boss strengths are the lever (G3's table in 5.2: about 10 to 13 points of boss win rate for 20 strength). The unaimed bars are met.
5. **The best strategy is 0.5 points under its ceiling.** Any easing will cross 75% (`towers_tall`) long before the 16 together reach 55% (they are at 50.4%). Ease through the other strategies, not the tower picks, or move the ceiling.

**Level designer**

6. **Two walls: `top_fork, mid_merge, bot_porch` (19.7%) and `top_twin, mid_merge, bot_porch` (11.9%).** L5's `mid_merge` caused them (F3: the old one gave 8.8% and 2.7% on the same plays), but the old one lost 10.3% on `top_fork, mid_merge, bot_square` (L5's number). Neither version passes all four stacks. Ideas to test with the bots, forcing each on seeds 1 to 200 (use `stack=` and `stop=6`): a third `mid_merge` drawing, or a `bot_porch` that moves its spots. On the stack the first gate adds 2 steps and no tower cover (47 to 47), unlike the stacks that pass.
7. **`mid_eye` is 0.78 times all runs** (bar 0.75) and `top_side` 0.80, on 30 and 26 seeds. Neither fails, and neither loses early. Before the next map change I would force the eight `mid_eye` stacks over full runs (about 1.5 hours with 3 processes). It only needs doing if someone changes a map.
8. Packs are clear. `b4_field_nurses` (9.9% natural, 8.7% forced) is the closest; do not cut it further unless a card change makes runs harder.

**Balance analyst (me, next time)**

9. Natural seeds give a stack 2 to 10 seeds and missed one wall entirely. Phase C of the job list (`run_b4.sh C`) now forces all 42 stacks onto seeds 1 to 200 in about 2 hours with 3 processes. I would run it after any segment change.
10. A bot that aims at Strong in the boss wave, sells and rebuilds, and reads the preview would make the bars closer to a player's. `boss_aim=1` is the first step; the other two are not built.

## Limits of these numbers

- The bots are a floor and play a fixed rule. They never aim (unless `boss_aim=1`), sell to rebuild, or read the preview. Real players should do better than the bars.
- Win rates depend on the number of seeds. 3,200 plays gives a standard error of about 0.9 points on the 16 together, 3.1 points on one strategy over 200 runs, 3 points on one boss pack, and 1.0 to 2.0 points on one card in the experiment. The what-if rows use 1,600 plays on the same seeds as the main run, so their differences are paired and tighter than that.
- The forced stack runs stop at wave 6 and measure only early losses. A stack can still be a wall later.
- The perk fit rule is mine: five pairs (Volley and Giant Bow, Twin Pebbles and Stone Sling, Long Fuse and Skyrocket, Frost Chime and Night Bell, Spice Rack and Grand Oven). The first three are TUNING_RUNS 3.5's examples. The last two are mine. A different rule could give a different result, but the pass-everything what-if (+4.5 points over the main run, still -8.0 against base) bounds what any fit rule can do.
- "From wave 9 on" for the Bakery is read as G3's hook read it: the bot builds a Bakery once 9 waves have started.
