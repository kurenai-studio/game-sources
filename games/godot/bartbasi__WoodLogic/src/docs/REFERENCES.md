# Reference games

Owner: game-designer. Phase 1.

These are references for play, not templates. Everything below comes from the public text
descriptions on the stores, read on 2026-10-19, and is written in my own words. I did not open
screenshots, videos or any other files, and nothing here (names, layouts, UI, text) goes into
WoodLogic. Where a description says nothing about a mechanic, I say so instead of guessing.

## Screwdom (primary reference)

**What the description says.** Colored screws are taken off pins and objects and sorted into
the box of their color. Order matters: one careless move can block you, so you plan. There is
no time limit and you play at your own pace. Many levels unlock in sequence and get trickier,
with themed models (it names a house, a duck and a cube). It leans on satisfying click sounds
for a calm, stress relieving mood. The description does not mention a holding tray, layers or
boosters, although the brief says the game has them.

**What it does well.** A loop that is understood in one tap: pick a screw, it goes to its box.
Calm pacing with steady, small rises in difficulty. The object being taken apart is a
recognizable thing, which gives every level a small "what is it" pleasure. Sound is part of the
reward.

**What we take.** The core loop (tap a screw, it flies to its color's box), the steady pacing,
recognizable silhouettes as level shapes (our blueprints), and sound as part of the reward of
every tap.

**What we leave out, and why.** Its business model (ads, purchases, lives) is against our
ground rules. Its 3D models: our board is flat 2.5D wood, which keeps every screw visible and
every touch target fixed. Its look, names and level layouts, which we must not copy.

## Screw Jam

**What the description says.** You unscrew wooden puzzles and sort screws, pins and nuts by
color. Levels have several layers and screws must come out in the right order. Some levels add
moving parts (rotating platforms, sliding pins). There are unlockable screw tools, an endless
mode after the main levels, and daily challenges with rewards.

**What it does well.** Layers turn sorting into an ordering puzzle: you must think about what
is on top. A daily challenge gives a reason to come back. Variety comes from new mechanics
mixed into familiar levels.

**What we take.** Layered planks where order matters, the box queue (the brief's take on its
target boxes), and the idea of a daily puzzle. Endless play, which our generator gives us by
design.

**What we leave out, and why.** Hidden layers: in WoodLogic every screw shows through the wood,
so every loss traces back to something the player could see. Rotating platforms and sliding
pins: more systems than the brief allows, and they would make the rest angle no longer a
simple function of geometry. Tool collecting and rewards for coming back: our only meta is
stars, helpers and themes.

## Screw Sort Puzzle (stand-in: Screw Sort Puzzle: Pin Jam 3D)

**What the description says.** Tap to collect colored screws and sort them by color; slots
clear as you solve, until the board is empty. It says there are no timers and no complex rules
or pressure, and finished levels unlock small 3D characters. It does not describe a holding
area, layers, boosters or a way to lose.

**What it does well.** Extreme simplicity: one verb, instant feedback, a board that visibly
empties. It promises the player can stop at any time.

**What we take.** The brief takes speed from the WebBest game, but the listing I could read
says nothing about a clock. Our decision stands on its own merits: speed only ever earns a
bonus star (the par time), it never loses a level, and the timer starts at the first tap so
reading the board is free.

**What we leave out, and why.** Collectible characters (extra systems), and anything that
makes the clock a threat.

## Happy Screw Trip 3D

**What the description says.** A 3D sorting puzzle with an ASMR theme. You study screws spread
over pillars, plan, and move them into matching color zones while working around obstacles.
Planning matters because a careless move can deadlock you. It names "capacity" and
"multi slot" tools that expand storage and layouts, flexible difficulty, no timers, drag and
rotate controls, more than a hundred stages with randomized elements, and calm sound with a
minimal look.

**What it does well.** It names the real fun of the genre plainly: look first, plan, avoid the
deadlock. It treats storage space as the resource that tools add to. It offers difficulty for
both casual players and puzzle fans, and uses randomness for replay.

**What we take.** "Look first, then act" as the heart of play, which is why our timer waits
for the first tap. Storage as the pressure resource: our Extra slot helper adds one tray slot.
Separate difficulty choices for players who want to unwind (our Relaxed mode with easy, medium
and hard). Generated variety on top of hand-made shapes.

**What we leave out, and why.** Dragging and rotating the view: we only tap, which keeps the
game one-handed and the board flat. Pillars and color zones: our destination is the box row
and the tray.

## Wood Nuts & Bolts style

**What the descriptions say.** (1) Wooden plates are pinned to a board by wooden bolts; you work
out the sequence that frees every plate, and puzzles may have more than one solution, with a
small hint system and hundreds of levels from beginner to advanced. (2) Simple twisting
controls with physics, and the boards drop as fasteners come out. (3) Tap to unscrew, and the
wooden plates pinned to the board fall away one at a time until the board is clear; levels get
trickier as you go. None of the three mentions a time limit.

**What they do well.** The wood itself is the reward: plates that drop with weight, wooden
sounds, a tactile, unhurried feel. "Many solutions, find the neat one" is a gentle kind of
challenge.

**What we take.** Planks that swing on their last screw and fall when the last one is gone,
wooden knocks and creaks, no time limit that loses a level, and the idea that a level has many
solutions with a cleaner one to find (our tray par and the Clean star).

**What we leave out, and why.** Moving screws from hole to hole (their main verb): our screws
leave the board for boxes, which is a different and simpler puzzle. Physics that decides the
outcome: in WoodLogic the rest angle comes from geometry, and physics is for looks only, so
the solver and the player always agree. Leaderboards, skins for sale and hint packs: online
and paid features are out by our ground rules. Themes in WoodLogic are earned by playing.

## What WoodLogic adds that none of the descriptions mention

- Every level is solved by the game before you see it.
- Every screw is always visible, even under wood, with its color and symbol.
- Colorblind symbols on by default.
- Free, with no ads, tracking or internet.
