Gator Hunt

Privacy Policy · Effective September 14, 2026

Gator Hunt collects nothing.

No personal data, no device identifiers, no analytics, no ads, no network access. Everything below explains exactly what that means and why.

What the app does

Gator Hunt is a single-player arcade game: alligators cross the screen and you tap to shoot them before too many get away. It runs entirely on your device, offline, from the moment it opens.

What we collect

Nothing. Gator Hunt doesn't ask for your name, email, location, photos, contacts, or any other personal information, and it has no account, login, or sign-up of any kind to collect that information through.

Permissions and access

Gator Hunt requests zero Android permissions — not camera, not storage, not even internet access. The app cannot reach the network, so nothing it does could be transmitted anywhere, even if it wanted to.

Where your score goes

Your score, shots fired, and kill count live in memory for the length of one play session. Closing the app or starting a new game clears them. Nothing is written to a save file, a server, or anywhere outside that session.

Third parties

None are involved. No advertising network, no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no social login. The game ships with exactly the code it takes to run the game — nothing that phones home, because there's nowhere for it to phone.

Children's privacy

Because Gator Hunt collects no data from anyone, it collects no data from children either. The game is suitable for players of any age on that basis.

Changes to this policy

If a future update ever changes what Gator Hunt does with data, this page will be updated first and the effective date above will change. As of that date, it hasn't, and the answer is still: nothing.
