THE DECK, BOTH SUITS
thirteen drawings, twenty-six creatures. Top row ♣ THE ROT, bottom ♠ THE COLD — same pixels, corruption layer swapped step for step — except the Ace, which is drawn twice.
THE ONE RULE
Threat must be legible from silhouette alone. A player scans four cards in about a second and never reads the number first — so how much of the 64×64 box the creature fills rises monotonically with its value. A 2 crouches in the bottom third. An Ace breaks the top guide. Get that right and the art is doing the rules' work.
MATERIALS, THEN CORRUPTION
A creature is made of its own materials — fur, flesh, bone, chitin, iron. The suit ramp is corruption growing on it, in patches and blooms, never the substance of it. A cellar rat is a rat with rot in its fur, not a green rat. Corruption has to be specified as an area, though — a quarter of the body in one or two blooms with crusted edges. Asked for loosely, it comes back as scattered single pixels that read as dirt.
Every ramp shifts hue, not just brightness. Eight steps each now, shadows running cool and violet, midtones saturated, lights warm — a ramp that only darkens renders as mud, which is what thin colour separation actually looks like. The secondary material stays clamped to its dark steps: give flesh its light step next to fur and flesh wins the body, because the lightest hex in the list always does.
But the master palette is only half of it. What reaches a sprite is the Force colors list, now 14 hexes — the full eight-step primary, three secondary, three corruption. And the dial that decides how many of those actually get used is Shading, not the palette: on Medium the model reaches for about four tones and ignores the rest, which is why the last rat came back flat with ten colours available. Set Shading to the highest option offered. Clubs and spades are mechanically identical, so they share every silhouette and swap only that corruption layer — thirteen drawings, twenty-six creatures, and the swap stays believable.

ALL THIRTEEN, ANIMATED
All twenty-six cards below are playing real five-frame cycles drawn in PixelLab, at 6fps. Fourteen uploads got there: thirteen clubs cycles plus a fourteenth for the ♠ Ace, which is a separate drawing and needed its own. The hurt frame is still generated for all of them — body lifts a pixel, every colour steps two up its own ramp, silhouette rimmed in cream.
Every frame that arrives gets measured against frame 1's own tone list and snapped back onto it — the generator drifts 2–300 px per frame, and unsnapped drift reads as flicker. The spades version is derived, never generated: the clubs→cold substitution is read pixel-by-pixel off each creature's approved pair, so whatever hex you actually used is what gets swapped. The Debt is the exception: its two suits are separate drawings, so there is no substitution to derive and each suit was animated separately. Its frames are also the only ones taken verbatim — 55–64 tones against the set's usual 15, richer than its neighbours on purpose.
THE HANDOFF
One upload per frame, clubs only. Frame 1 must be the approved sprite unchanged — it is the authority every other frame is measured against, and so far it has matched to the pixel every time. Frames 2–5 get snapped and the spades set is derived. All twenty-six cards are in. Hurt and death frames are the remaining work.
