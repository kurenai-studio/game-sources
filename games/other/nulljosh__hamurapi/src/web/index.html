How it plays

Land, grain and people

You get 100 people, 1,000 acres and a barn full of grain. Each year you choose how much land to buy, how much to feed your people and how much to plant. Then you watch what happens.

City

It all shows

Good harvest and the crowd cheers. Rats run at the barn. Starve people and you see the ghosts, and the graves stay.

Story

A story with choices

Traders, floods, a greedy king next door, a woman who reads the stars. Ten events, two choices each.

Daily

Today's game

Everyone who plays today gets the same luck. Same harvests, same rats. Compare grades.

Classic

The 1968 game, untouched

Classic mode is the original rules with nothing added. Three levels for the story.

Robot

Watch the robot

A robot king plays a whole game while you watch. It gets an A+ in 878 of 1,000 games. Beat it.

Music

Music made of math

The tune and every sound are worked out by a short program. No recordings.

Where it came from

Thank you to the first ones

In 1964 Mabel Addis and William McKay made The Sumer Game for school kids. In 1968 Doug Dyment wrote Hamurabi on a computer with almost no memory. In 1973 David Ahl put it in 101 BASIC Computer Games. This one is a fresh build on the 1968 rules. The code, art, music and story are new, and open under the MIT License.
