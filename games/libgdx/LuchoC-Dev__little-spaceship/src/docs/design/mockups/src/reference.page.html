The sheet you keep open while drawing

Not a validation of the direction — that is what the combat screen is for. This is the working reference: the 32 colours with the measurements that make them a system, every sprite at the size it must be drawn at with its collider on top, the six silhouettes in flat black, and both bitmap fonts glyph by glyph. Everything is generated from the same source the game will be drawn from, so this sheet cannot drift away from 02-sprite-sizes.md.

Sizes and colliders

Sprite dimensions are exact and every one of them is odd, so a centred sprite has its axis of symmetry on one pixel column. Radii are what the code collides with: Collider is a circle with no offset, so the visual mass belongs at the centre and anything outside the circle has to read as secondary.

Silhouettes

R16, filled with flat black. If two archetypes are hard to tell apart here, they are hard to tell apart in play — where they are smaller, moving, and surrounded by things trying to kill you.

The palette

Two disjoint sets and a reserved band. L* is CIE lightness: backgrounds stop at 44.9 and gameplay starts at 48.1, which is the gap that makes a bullet impossible to lose against any legal background.

Ramps

Shading moves along a ramp; it does not invent colour. A sprite that needs a tone between two of these is a sprite that needs to be simplified, not a palette that needs a new entry.

Typography

Both fonts are drawn for this project, white on transparent, tinted at draw time. Fixed advance in both, which is why every number is tabular for free.

What the checks say

    Generated from docs/design/mockups/src/ by build.py, verified by check.js. Silhouettes stand in for art that has not been drawn: what is exact here is the footprint, the palette and the metrics.
