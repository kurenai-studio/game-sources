# Critic: Phase 2 world (shots/p2)

VERDICT: FAIL

Frames reviewed: all golden-hour frames (`ground`, `50m`, `200m`, `500m`, `down`, `basket`, `river`, `lake`), plus `200m_sunset`, `500m_sunrise` and `down_blue`.
Compared with: `refs/aerial_combe_village.jpg`, `aerial_sampford_church.jpg`, `aerial_donkmeer_lake.jpg`, `aerial_quy_road_fields.jpg`, Summer Cycle `refs/user_village_field.jpg` and `media/03-sunset-paddies.jpg`.

The patchwork is a real start. From 200–500 m the world reads as a pleasant board-game map, not yet as a hand-painted miniature. The village, which matters most, falls apart at the two views the brief calls "the whole game": `down` and `basket`.

## Problems (most important first)

1. **down.png / basket.png: the village is sparse boxes on an empty lawn.** About 60% of the `down` frame is one flat olive field. Houses sit one by one at even spacing along the roads, with big bare gaps between them, and there is no roofscape. `aerial_sampford_church.jpg` shows the opposite. Roofs touch, terraces join, and outbuildings, sheds, walls, paths, yards, veg plots and dense garden trees fill every gap. Cluster the heart tightly, with joined terraces, back extensions and lean-tos. Fill the land behind the streets with gardens, orchards and paddocks that have texture. Do not leave plain lawn.
2. **down.png / basket.png: the garden plots are empty black wire rectangles.** The fences read as thin black outlines floating on the grass. Several plots have no house at all (`down.png` top-right, x≈1020–1130, y≈280–350). Others are offset from their house, and some are a bare brown square. Real plots are bounded by hedges, low walls or picket fences in warm colours. They should hold lawn stripes, veg rows, a shed, a path and a tree. The outline should be dark green or stone, not ink-black. A plot with no house must not exist.
3. **All aerial frames: the haze is a muddy khaki/ochre filter, not luminous golden air.** The sky is one flat mustard-brown wash with no gradient towards the sun, and the paper overlay makes it look dirty. Over the land the haze lifts the darks and turns every green olive-grey (see the horizon band in `200m.png` and `500m.png`). `aerial_combe_village.jpg` has amber light with deep shadow. Summer Cycle `03-sunset-paddies.jpg` has a clean, saturated peach-to-rose gradient that glows near the sun. Push the haze towards warm peach/cream: bright near the sun and cooler blue-lilac in the far shadows. Keep the foreground blacks deep.
4. **200m.png / 500m.png / lake.png: trees are not hazed with the ground.** Lone trees and hedge trees are bright lime spheres that keep full saturation at every distance. The field under them is hazed, so they sit on top like stickers. The vegetation has to get exactly the same aerial-perspective fog as the terrain. The trees are also all the same size, colour and round shape; mix in darker greens, ochre-tinged crowns and varied sizes.
5. **down_blue.png: an artefact.** Long pale streaks and smears cross the whole ground diagonally, like shadow-map or reflection stretching. Fix it before anything else ships. In `down.png` the green also shows ghostly translucent circles, and the top-left has a hard-edged dark wedge with straight sides (a shadow-frustum or hill-shadow edge?).
6. **200m.png / 500m.png: the river is a ruled canal.** It runs almost dead straight across the frame at a constant width, with no meanders, oxbows or variation in bank vegetation. The brief asks for a winding river. Give it real meanders (2–4 bends visible at 500 m), a varying width, willow or alder lines that follow the bends, and gravel or mud banks on the inside of each bend.
7. **500m.png: you cannot tell the village is there.** It is a faint reddish smudge about 150 px wide, lost in fields with the same red/brown hues, and the church tower cannot be seen. The windmill is invisible at 500 m and an unreadable 5 px speck at 200 m (`200m.png`, x≈210, y≈360). These are the landmarks of the whole map. Use warmer, brighter roofs, add the church spire's shadow, and keep these silhouettes readable at altitude (slight scale bias or an LOD that keeps the spire and sails). The windmill should sit on a visibly bald hilltop.
8. **Water (lake.png, river.png, 50m.png): cold slate blue under a warm sky, and the ripples tile.** In `aerial_donkmeer_lake.jpg` the lake is a mirror of the pale warm sky. Here it is cool blue-grey with a busy repeating checker of light ripples, which looks like a texture tile, especially on the river in `50m.png` and `river.png`. The reflection should be mostly the peach sky, with a darker band under the banks and only faint ripples. Make the sun glitter a single path, not a pattern all over.
9. **lake.png: the shoreline is a clean blob with a pale lip.** Arable fields run straight to the water's edge, with no reeds, no shore trees on most of the margin, no jetty and no muddy margin. The "reed fringe" in PROGRESS is not visible. Add reed beds, alder/willow clumps and a few broken shore sections. The edge should look grown, not drawn.
10. **down.png / basket.png: the shadows don't agree.** The trees cast huge, long dark ellipses that dominate the frame. The houses cast almost nothing, and the church shadow is weak. At golden hour every object casts a long shadow; the houses need gable-shaped shadows as long as the trees'. The tree shadows also look like solid blobs; they should be broken and dappled at the edges.
11. **down.png / basket.png: the church does not read.** From straight down it is a long grey box with a smudge. In `basket` it stands alone on open lawn, with no churchyard wall, path, yew trees or graves. Compare the churchyard in `aerial_sampford_church.jpg`. Add a walled churchyard, a lychgate path to the street, dark yews and the tower's long shadow. Give the nave a slate roof that is clearly different from the cottages' clay.
12. **basket.png / down.png: the bridge and roads.** In `basket` the bridge is a flat tan plank lying at an angle to the road, with no visible arch or parapet. In `down` it is a thin strip. The road centre at the green is a soft, blurry tan blob with smeared edges. Roads need a crisp edge, verges, a darker wheel-worn centre and a defined junction. The bridge must line up with the road and show its parapets from above.
13. **ground.png / 50m.png / 200m.png foreground: large flat empty fields.** The lower half of `ground.png` is one flat green plane. The near fields in `50m.png` and `200m.png` have no grass strokes, tussocks, mow lines or colour variation. The Summer Cycle frames get their charm from painted grass detail in the foreground. Add painted stroke texture and low-frequency colour patches (`aerial_quy_road_fields.jpg` shows strong tonal variation inside single fields).
14. **The ink lines are too heavy and too uniform.** Hedges are drawn as near-black lines of the same weight in the foreground and in the middle distance. At 200–500 m this turns into a black grid that reads like a map or a vector drawing. Let the outline fade and warm with distance, and give hedges gaps, gates and hedge trees at irregular spacing.
15. **The backdrop is stage flats.** In `200m.png` and `500m.png` the terrain ends in a pale fog band, then a band of olive lumpy "tree" ridges, then flat cut-out blue mountains. It is the same in every frame, so it reads as a painted backdrop. The clouds are faceted grey-white lumps with dark outlines; they sit low on the horizon and visibly merge into the ground haze (`500m.png`, left). Blend the terrain into the ridges and lift the clouds into the sky with soft lit undersides. Make the far mountains a warm lilac haze tone, not saturated blue.
16. **Animals and smoke.** The sheep in `200m.png` (bottom-right) are brown pellets that are too large and the wrong colour; most sheep should read as cream dots. Chimney smoke in `50m.png` and `200m.png` is a set of grey horizontal dashes; it should be a pale, sun-lit, slightly blue wisp that rises before it bends.

## What works
- The patchwork palette at 200–500 m: gold, olive, emerald, lavender and plough-brown parcels on turning grains. It is the right idea and already reads as farmland.
- `river.png`: the stone arch bridge, the clay-tile roofs with chimneys and the painted foliage crowns are in the right stylised register.
- The golden-hour long shadows of hedges and trees over the fields give good depth at 200 m.
- Orchards in rows and woods with ragged edges add good texture variety in the middle distance.
- The sunset variant (`200m_sunset.png`) shifts the palette convincingly towards rose and violet.
