# Decisions, 2026-10-31 onward

| Date | Decision |

| 2026-10-31 | **A first native iOS prototype** (the user: "a real native iOS app, not just a web app in the shell of an app... start with something small like a simple battle map with a few characters and different elements and the core party piece"). Swift + SpriteKit, as tech.md §13 planned, in `ios/` (XcodeGen, bundle `com.jonathancox.ninthtower`; landscape, 120 Hz on ProMotion). **The pipeline it proves:** the art is exported, not redrawn: `tools/native-export.js` runs in the dev page, boots the Training Grounds (`arena`) and writes every sheet the slice draws (the party's baked gear sheets and portraits, five foes, effects, icons, the pixel font) with its frames and animations, the map's painted ground, its 399 props as placed (with their depth) and its walkable grid, and renders 22 sound effects through the web synth offline; `node tools/native-sync.mjs` unpacks that into `ios/NinthTower/Export/` (1 MB) with heroes, enemies, skills, items and tuning.json copied unchanged. **What's ported** (line for line where it could be, in the web's own space, pixels with y down): the leader's commands and idle attacks, the companions' targeting by role, formation follow, flanking, ranged spacing and firing spots, dodging locked telegraphs, reviving; foes noticing, surrounding with two attack tokens, wind-ups that track then lock, archers keeping away and shifting after a shot; damage, mitigation, crits, evasion and the counter, elements (weak/resist, WEAK!, burn, poison, chill stacking to freeze); Brann's Bash and Taunt, Pip's Fireball and Mend, Sable's Shadow Step and Fan of Knives; the three ultimates; hit-stop, shake, the game speed (0.72), A* with string-pulling. **The battle:** waves on the Training Grounds, each a mix of elements (goblins and an archer; the frost pack; ember imps and gloom spiders; all of them), foes two levels up each round, a wipe retries the wave. **Native-only:** tap to move or attack, flick to dodge, hold and drag to steer, tap a portrait to lead with that hero; haptics on the leader's hits, crits, blows taken, ultimates and a fall; sounds through AVAudioEngine (it follows the silent switch). **Checked:** built and run in the simulator (iPhone 17 Pro): four waves fought through with the companions on their own, the portrait swap (Pip led with his own buttons; Brann used Bulwark as a companion), a flicked dodge; installed on the user's iPhone 15 Pro Max and launched. **Not checked:** play by hand on the phone (the user is testing it), frame times on the device, the steering drag. **Left out of the slice:** music, saves, gear, levels and XP, the other heroes and foes, the world. |
| 2026-10-31 | **The native prototype: a performance readout, and its first hitches found.** The user asked how to test performance. A two-finger tap shows frames a second against the screen's rate, average and worst frame time, late frames (half again the budget), the game's own update time and nodes; +20 FOES adds a mixed pack to push it. Launched with `-perfLog` (`xcrun devicectl device process launch --console ... -perfLog`) it prints the same every two seconds and adds three packs, so a run on the phone reads on the Mac. **Measured on the user's iPhone 15 Pro Max (Release):** 120 fps steady with the update under 0.5 ms, even at 42 foes, but single frames of 40-90 ms (once 322 ms) in busy fighting; the user's own screenshot showed 92 of 120 with a 53 ms worst. A Time Profiler trace (Instruments, 41 s) put the game's logic at 4.6% of the main thread and rendering at about 6.5%, with no long running update: the spikes were waits. **Fixed, and measured again on the phone (46 s, packs up to 60 foes):** 120 fps held throughout, the update averaging 0.1-1.6 ms with a worst of 5.8 ms, and 5 late frames in all, every one in the two seconds after the first pack of frost wolves, imps and spiders joined (31-42 ms: something done the first time they fight; not yet found). Before the fix the same run had 120 late frames and a 322 ms worst. The fix: each sound stopped the voice it reused with `AVAudioPlayerNode.stop()`, which makes the main thread wait for the audio thread; a sound now cuts in with `.interrupts`. Also every sheet loads at start (a first load mid-fight cost 5-17 ms) and HUD text is rebuilt only when it changes. **Not done:** foes surrounding a hero check every other foe (`surroundSlot`), the biggest steady cost in the logic; fine at 40 foes. |
| 2026-10-31 | **The native prototype: the last hitch found and fixed.** The user: "Find out what it is." The five late frames left (31-42 ms, just after the first pack of wolves, imps and spiders joined) were sounds: a probe that names any slow piece of a frame (`Probe` in Util.swift, on only with `-perfLog`; it also times each sound and haptic) showed every sound taking 8-22 ms in two windows, the first sound of the game and the first burst of fighting, and never after. Each of the 14 voices set up its audio processing on its first `play()`, so the first 14 sounds each paid for it, several in one frame. All voices now start at launch, idling on silence. **Measured on the phone (40 s, packs up to 60 foes):** no late frames at all, 120 fps throughout, the update's worst 5.5 ms (twenty foes appearing at once, with their puffs; inside the 8.3 ms a frame has). |
| 2026-10-31 | **A plan to finish the native iOS game** ([plans/native-ios.md](../plans/native-ios.md)), asked for by the user after the prototype. Art is exported from the web build (it all is, but the heroes in their gear, whose composer is ported and diffed pixel for pixel); the rules are ported and checked against the web's own functions run in JavaScriptCore; the screens are rebuilt in SpriteKit. The web stays the design lab, with a port ledger marking ported systems the web has since changed. Seven phases, each playable on the phone: groundwork, every fight, the world, the RPG, quests and dungeons, music, the platform, shipping. Five decisions are the user's, first among them whether the native code stays in the public MIT repository of a paid app. |
| 2026-10-31 | **The native game goes private; the port starts.** The user's answers to the plan's decisions: the native code moves to a private repository (`maxwellimpact/ninth-tower-ios`, created and pushed; `ios/` removed here, though it stays in this repository's history), iPhone and iPad, no save import from the web for now, and the design frozen while the port runs. `tools/native-sync.mjs` now writes into `../ninth-tower-ios` (or `NATIVE_DIR`). From here the native work is logged in that repository's own `DECISIONS.md`; rows here only when the web side changes. |
| 2026-10-31 | **The native export grows: music, gear, gear references.** `tools/native-export.js` gains `music()` (each song's six layers rendered one loop each through the web's own sequencer, offline; `{only:[ids]}` re-renders some), `gear()` (the paper-doll data the native composer draws from) and `gearRef()` (reference sheets drawn by the web's puppet code: the native composer matched all 124 pixel for pixel); `native-sync.mjs` copies music and gear data. Tools only: the game is unchanged. Noticed, left alone: the Proving Field (`?map=battlefield`) has one curse blight patch (`blight2`, east of the camp), since `buildBlight` reads its regions as Aldermere's. |
| 2026-11-01 | **Nothing a foe starts outlives it** (never-again rule 17). The user saw attacks outlive a foe in the native app; a new native test (`-smoke afterlife`: every foe and boss on the Proving Field killed mid-attack) found two, and both were in this code too. An Avalanche Troll killed mid ground-pound left its six ice-spike circles, its centre ring and their danger zones for good: `endPound()` now clears them on destroy. A Fire Beetle that lit its fuse during a swing's wind-up left that swing's warning and zone behind: `aiBomber` now clears the old zone first. Tested in the browser: a troll killed 0.6 s into its pound leaves no zone or drawing; a beetle wound up, then fused, keeps one zone and leaves none after its blast. No console errors. The native app has the same fixes (all 47 foes and bosses pass there). |
| 2026-11-01 | **The native app paints its own ground**, so `tools/native-sync.mjs` now copies the exported terrain chunks to the native repository's `reference/terrain/<id>/` (a pixel reference for its `-terraintest`) instead of into the app's Export folder; curses and vines still go into the app. No change to the web game. |
| 2026-11-01 | **The native app paints its vines and curse overlays too**, so `tools/native-sync.mjs` sends the exported vine and curse pictures to the native repository's `reference/vines` and `reference/curses` (for its checks) instead of into the app. No change to the web game. |
| 2026-11-01 | **Coins sound again; a true crossfade between regions; never-again rule 18.** The user missed the coin's sound in the native port. Since d7669c4 (2026-10-16) a comment added mid-line had swallowed the coin's sound, sparkle and HUD update; a scan found the chain's countdown label lost the same way. Both restored, and `tools/comment-check.mjs` now checks for it. The design says the music crossfades at a border, but the old song faded out and only then did the new one begin. Now the new song starts at once and fades in (time constant 0.5 s) while the old one sequences on and fades out beneath it. Tested in the browser, muted: a coin adds its gold, sound and HUD event; the chain's label counts down; the two gains sum to 1 throughout, the blend is about done by 2 s, the old song's gains are released at 6.5 s; no console errors. Not heard by me: two songs overlapping for a moment need the user's ears. |
