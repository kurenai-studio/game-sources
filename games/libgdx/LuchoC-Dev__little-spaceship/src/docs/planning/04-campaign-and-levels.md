# Campaign, narrative and levels

## General structure

The first campaign will have five stages. Each stage aims for **3–5 levels**, which produces an indicative campaign of 15–25 levels. The range is a guide: a stage must not be padded artificially if it already fulfils its function.

Most levels will end with a boss. Valid exceptions:

- narrative or animated level;
- large wave;
- chase;
- prolonged defence;
- special event without a traditional boss.

Also contemplated:

- sub-bosses in specific levels;
- multi-boss levels;
- an intermediate boss followed by new waves and another boss;
- different or modified bosses in alternative modes.

## Stage 1 — Invasion of Earth

### Narrative function

Present the first big alien wave and the inability of conventional military technology to respond. An experimental human ship proves that fighting the invaders is possible.

### Possible settings

- Cities.
- Secret or military bases.
- Strategic infrastructure.
- Mountains or terrestrial relief.
- Buildings and defences.
- Meteorites or debris caused by the invasion.

### Evolution

It begins with simple alien units and small formations. Coordination, variety and pressure increase as the stage advances.

### Stage closing

Destruction of a large mothership, which represents a first important victory and pushes the conflict towards orbit.

## Stage 2 — Earth orbit

The war moves to the upper atmosphere and near space:

- satellites;
- stations;
- orbital debris;
- fleets;
- defending and regaining control around Earth.

The setting must be distinguishable from the surface and allow threats/obstacles specific to space.

## Stage 3 — Defence of the Moon

Humanity protects lunar installations or strategic positions. Alien ships still predominate, but hints of a stranger nature behind the invasion may appear.

### Narrative transition

During the return towards Earth or an important battle, a supership appears. When destroyed, a giant alien or entity emerges from inside it. This revelation works as an act change.

## Stage 4 — War against the entities

The conflict continues in space, but the enemies stop looking merely like vehicles:

- space creatures;
- biomechanical organisms;
- entities that function as ships;
- organic structures;
- hybrids of technology and biology;
- enemies of enormous scale.

Conventional ships may still coexist with these entities. The stage reveals the true dimension of the invading force.

## Stage 5 — Last defence of Earth

The evolved entities force humanity to fall back. The campaign returns to Earth, but with a different tone:

- Stage 1: “we are being invaded”.
- Stage 5: “this is the last line of defence”.

Earth must visually reflect the wear and the escalation of the war. The final victory closes this campaign satisfactorily.

## Potential continuation

After surviving, humanity stops limiting itself to defence. The final scene may open a second, offensive campaign:

**Solar System → alien territory → enemy planet/origin.**

It is not part of the current scope and does not need to be designed yet.

## Narrative and presentation between stages

The format is still open. Alternatives contemplated:

- brief cutscenes;
- pixel-art images with text;
- radio communications;
- mission briefing;
- scenes inside the hangar;
- environmental events during gameplay itself.

For the MVP, environmental narrative is favoured: human aircraft, defences, explosions, ships in the background and signs of a larger war, avoiding escort missions or NPC management.

## Level 1 design

### Premise

An experimental ship takes off in a hurry from a secret base to defend a city or important location during the first wave. Human aircraft and defences try to help, but alien technology outmatches them.

### Objective

Contain and clear the attack zone, finally defeating the large ship that leads or accompanies the wave. It is a local victory, not the end of the invasion.

### Provisional sequence

- Audiovisual introduction.
- Initial calm to establish the setting.
- First isolated basic enemies.
- Light/fast enemies.
- Combined formations.
- Tanks and shifts in priority.
- Super-fast ones.
- One or two heavy carriers.
- Evolved basics/shooters.
- High-pressure combinations.
- A difficult encounter. Defeating it delivers the level's attachment.
- Brief rest.
- Final escalation.
- Boss.

It is not a rigid script. It will be adjusted through the intensity curve and playtesting.

## Environmental obstacles and threats

They depend on the setting:

- Earth: buildings, mountains, defences, debris and meteorites from the attack.
- Orbit/space: satellites, stations, debris and space bodies.
- Moon: lunar relief and infrastructure.
- Organic stages: living or biomechanical structures.

In the MVP the background scenario is decorative and does not collide: it communicates the invasion without taking up playable space. The only exception is a few **destructible structures**, which are valid targets and drop resources when broken.

The behaviour of solid obstacles —blocking, contact damage or spatial pressure— is left for later stages, when level design can support it.
